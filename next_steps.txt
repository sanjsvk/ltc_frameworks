What it is: Explain WHY each framework fails/succeeds in specific scenarios; establish causal links between architecture and performance

Paper points:
  1. MCMC divergences diagnostic: Solver issue not data structure (fixed by target_accept 0.99, tune 1500; 0 divergences S1-S2)
     - Implication: Convergence metric validated; safe for deployment
  2. Kalman DLM S3 degradation: Missing explicit seasonal state component (architectural trade-off, not bug)
     - Implication: Use BSTS for seasonal data, Kalman for non-seasonal; not a failure

     - Implication: Weibull unsuitable for multi-channel LTC; design limitation

     - Mechanism: Single decay per channel insufficient to resolve temporal heterogeneity

     - Mechanism: AR coefficients absorb seasonal noise, inverting hierarchy
     - Implication: Channel validation critical; aggregate metrics insufficient
  6. F3 state-space structural robustness: Fixed decay brittle on weak signal but Bayesian flexibility (MCMC) recovers
     - Mechanism: State-space structure constrains estimation; MCMC prior tuning activates flexibility
     - Implication: MCMC as production-ready when priors calibrated
  7. Video LTC test: Only MCMC recovers δ_video (0.88) vs δ_tv (0.90) distinction; differentiator for channel heterogeneity
     - Implication: Bayesian uncertainty quantification enables fine-grained channel separation
  8. S5 signal threshold: Calibration boundary (not architectural); MCMC 88.5% with tuning vs 0% frozen

## Section 10: Recommendations & Practitioner Guidance
What it is: Actionable framework selection and deployment guidance tied to real-world campaign characteristics

Documents referenced:
  - @STEP4_OPTIMIZATION_RESULTS.md — Practitioner guidance (high-value vs medium-value campaigns + signal assessment)
  - @paper_notes.md — Finding #8 (Signal threshold), Finding #11 (Prior specification)
  - @S5_SUPPLEMENTARY_BREAKTHROUGH.txt — Signal assessment workflow (obs_var, spend_cv, channel_independence)
  - Discussion section synthesis

Paper points:
  1. High-value campaigns (long-term, stable spend, ≥2 years data):
     - Recommend: F3 (bsts or mcmc_stock)
     - Why: Stable performance across scenarios, 2-3pp optimization margin, robust to calibration errors
     - Deployment: Minimal tuning; frozen parameters safe
     - Effort: 2-5 min optimization per scenario
  2. Medium-value campaigns (scenario monitoring feasible, 1-2 years data):
     - Recommend: F2 (koyck, ardl with L2 regularization)
     - Why: Moderate calibration sensitivity (5-6pp improvement available), benefits from scenario-specific tuning
     - Deployment: Scenario-specific parameter tuning required; monitor performance on hold-out periods
     - Effort: 1-2 min optimization per scenario
     - Risk: ARDL coefficient sign-flip in structural breaks (mitigate with L2 regularization)
  3. Avoid F1 unless:
     - Structural break detection in place (e.g., change-point detection monitoring)
     - Why: Minimal calibration benefit, high variance, unreliable channel attribution
     - Calibration cannot overcome architectural limitations
  4. Signal strength assessment workflow:
     - Compute obs_var / (stc_variance + ltc_variance) ratio
     - If ratio <0.5: Use MCMC with scenario-specific priors (high information content)
     - If ratio 0.5–2.0: Use F2 or F3 with normal tuning
     - If ratio >2.0: Use F1 or simple baseline (signal too weak; model cannot distinguish)
  5. Channel validation mandatory:
     - Always report per-channel recovery rates, not aggregate only
     - ARDL/koyck: High risk of inversion; validate against spend impact patterns
     - MCMC: High confidence in ranking; suitable for budget allocation

Expected length: 1.5 pages
Structure: Three campaign types + signal assessment workflow + channel validation checklist
