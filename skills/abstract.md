## Structure — Six Sentences

**Sentence 1 — The problem.**
What is wrong with current practice. One sentence, no jargon.

**Sentence 2 — Why it matters.**
The business consequence of the problem. Quantify if possible.

**Sentence 3 — What this paper does.**
The methodological contribution. One sentence.

**Sentence 4–5 — Key findings.**
Two to three specific findings with numbers. No hedging.

**Sentence 6 — Implication.**
What practitioners or researchers should do differently.

## Example Structure (fill in your numbers)

"Marketing mix models routinely underestimate long-term media contributions (LTC)
from sustained brand investment, leading to systematic misallocation of media budgets
toward short-term performance channels. We develop a reproducible benchmarking
framework using synthetic data with known ground truth to evaluate ten LTC estimation
methods across five diagnostic scenarios. State-space methods with Bayesian latent
stock estimation recover [X]% of true LTC on average across scenarios, compared to
[X]% for distributed lag models and [X]% for static adstock methods. Critically,
aggregate recovery metrics mask channel-level attribution failures — two methods
achieving [X]% aggregate recovery return 0% recovery for individual channels,
inverting budget allocation recommendations. We propose a three-tier robustness
taxonomy and provide a practitioner decision framework for method selection
based on signal strength and spend pattern characteristics."
