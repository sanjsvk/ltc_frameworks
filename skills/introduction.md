# sections/introduction.md
## Introduction

Target length: 800–1000 words. Four to five paragraphs.

---

## Paragraph Structure

**Paragraph 1 — Open with the business problem, not the method.**
Start broad. Why does LTC matter to marketers? What is the scale of the problem?
Anchor with a fact about MMM industry spend or media budget scale.
Do not mention adstock or state-space in paragraph 1.

**Paragraph 2 — The identification gap.**
What does current practice do? What does it miss?
Introduce adstock briefly. State the core failure: methods that tie LTC to spend
cannot detect LTC that persists after spend stops.
Cite 2–3 foundational MMM papers here.

**Paragraph 3 — Why existing benchmarks are insufficient.**
Most MMM validation uses aggregate metrics or real data where ground truth is unknown.
This paper uses synthetic data with known ground truth — the only way to measure
exact recovery. This is the methodological contribution framing.

**Paragraph 4 — What this paper contributes.**
List 3–4 specific contributions. Use numbered or bulleted list.
Be specific — name the scenarios, the taxonomy, the finding.
Do not say "novel" or "to the best of our knowledge."

Example contributions:
1. A reproducible synthetic data benchmarking framework with known LTC ground truth
2. Empirical evidence that aggregate LTC recovery masks channel-level attribution failure
3. A three-tier robustness taxonomy (Structural / Identification-Dependent / Fragile)
4. A practitioner decision framework for method selection based on signal and spend conditions

**Paragraph 5 — Paper roadmap.**
One sentence per section. Keep it mechanical.
"Section 2 reviews the literature on LTC estimation. Section 3 describes..."

---

## Tone Guidance for Introduction

JMR wants papers that make a clear theoretical, substantive, or methodological
contribution. State yours clearly by the end of paragraph 4.

Marketing Science values papers that answer important questions. Frame the
opening around the question: "How much LTC are brands missing, and why?"

Do not oversell. Do not undersell. State what you found and why it matters.

---

## Do Not Include in Introduction
- Results tables or figures
- Detailed methodology
- Literature review content (that goes in Section 2)
- Definitions of all terms (introduce key terms, define the critical ones)
