# sections/literature.md
## Literature Review

Target length: 1000–1400 words. Four subsections.

---

## Purpose

Position the paper against existing work. For each literature stream:
1. What has been established
2. What gap remains
3. How this paper addresses the gap

Every gap must connect to a specific contribution from the paper.

---

## Four Subsections

### 2.1 Adstock and Distributed Lag Models in MMM
Core papers: Clarke (1976), Broadbent (1979), Koyck (1954), Hanssens et al. (2001)
Cover: geometric adstock, Weibull/Almon shapes, dual adstock
Gap to identify: all these methods tie LTC to spend — fail when LTC persists
after spend stops, and under collinearity

### 2.2 State-Space and Latent Variable Approaches
Core papers: Harvey (1990), Durbin and Koopman (2012)
MMM applications: papers applying Kalman filter or BSTS to marketing
Gap to identify: limited benchmarking of state-space vs adstock for LTC specifically

### 2.3 Brand Equity and Long-Term Marketing Effects
Core papers: Keller (1993), Srinivasan and Hanssens (2009), Datta et al. (2017)
Cover: brand equity as latent stock concept, long-run advertising effects
Gap: most brand equity models use awareness or survey data, not MMM spend series

### 2.4 MMM Benchmarking and Validation
Core papers: Jin et al. (2017), Robyn/Meta papers, any synthetic benchmarking work
Gap: existing benchmarks use real data (unknown ground truth) or single-method
validation. No multi-method comparison using synthetic ground truth exists.
This is the paper's clearest gap to claim.

## Citation Targets

JMR and Marketing Science expect 25–40 references for a methodology paper.
IJRM has no stated limit but expects comprehensive literature coverage.

Minimum required citations per subsection:
- 2.1 Adstock: 8–10 citations
- 2.2 State-space: 5–7 citations
- 2.3 Brand equity: 5–7 citations
- 2.4 Benchmarking: 4–6 citations

---

## What to Avoid

- Do not summarise papers one by one — synthesise across papers
- Do not cite a paper without connecting it to the gap
- Do not claim a gap without citing evidence of what exists
- Do not end the section without a clear transition to Section 3 (Methodology)

---

## Closing Paragraph

Summarise the three gaps and state how the paper addresses each.
End with one sentence connecting the literature to the research question.
